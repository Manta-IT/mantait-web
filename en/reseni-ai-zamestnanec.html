One of its working days

By the time you unlock the office at seven, it has half a day behind it.

This is what a day looked like at a model trading company of twenty people. The numbers are an illustration, not a promise. At your company it will do what you decide.

2:10

It cleans up while the company sleeps

It went through 214 companies in the CRM and for 63 of them filled in the missing company ID, address or line of business from public registers. For 9 it found the company had changed its managing director and flagged them for review. It merged 4 duplicate records of the same company.

3:40

It picks up inquiries that came after closing time

It pulled 6 new inquiries from the mailbox, read the attachments, filled in the form and created them in the system. It recognized 2 as existing customers and attached them to their history. It marked one as spam.

5:20

It watches what got stuck

It found 11 quotes the customer had not responded to for a week and prepared a reminder for each one, in the style of the salesperson who sent it. They wait for his approval.

6:45

It checks the numbers

It compared the prices in the quotes against the current price list. In 3 it found a mismatch: twice an old price, once a discount beyond the rules. It flagged them and wrote down what it noticed.

7:30

It distributes the work

Every salesperson has today's tasks sorted by what is urgent and where the money is. The manager can see who has how much work in progress.

7:55

Your report

One page: what happened yesterday, what it handled, where it got stuck, and three things it needs you to decide. Nothing more.

13:12

It doesn't stop when you go to lunch

An inquiry that arrived at 13:12 is in the system at 13:13. At 14:40 it found that an e-mail to a customer bounced and told the salesperson who sent it.

18:30

It closes the day

It updated what changed during the day, prepared materials for tomorrow and listed what stayed open. Tomorrow at two in the morning it starts again.
