Case study

She wrote e-mails. Three weeks later her application was live.

A manufacturing company quoted jobs in an application built ten years ago and never updated since. One person in the company knew how to operate it. Today five people work in the new application and prices are calculated against current price lists.

20 days
from order to live operation at the company
The brief came from her answers.
The only thing she wrote were answers to my questions. She also sent the price lists she kept in Excel. I took care of the rest.

How it went

Three weeks, four rounds of feedback.

1

A free preview

First I built a simplified version that could only calculate a price, and sent a link. No invoice, no commitment. I wanted to know I was aiming right before we started talking about it.

2

The order and twelve Excel files

The client sent the price lists as she kept them: twelve spreadsheets for three production technologies. With them, the rules of her trade in plain sentences. No technical specification was ever written.

3

A new version the next day

She clicked through it, wrote down what didn't fit, and the next day she had an updated version. Four times in a row. Then deployment to the company network, accounts for each estimator and live operation.

What came out of it

Five people instead of one.

01

Calculation for three production technologies

Each with its own parameters, materials and rules. Purchase and selling prices including volume discounts.

02

Connected to the warehouse system

The application uses actual warehouse prices. When they differ from the price list by more than a set percentage, it raises a flag on its own.

03

Administration she handles herself

Price lists, discounts, help texts and default values: the client changes them without me. Every change can be traced.

04

The program checks its own numbers

Every calculation runs a second time, a different way. If the two numbers don't match, the application stops and shows where the difference is. So no estimator sends a customer a wrong price.

What it cost

A price she knew from the start.

 

1 650 EUR

The quote had a cap of 1 650 EUR. More got fine-tuned than we expected, including things that were not in the brief at all, and the cap did not move. I invoiced 1 650 EUR, exactly the amount the client had counted on from day one.

 

Nothing quietly added

Anything beyond the scope had a price in advance and the client decided whether she wanted it. Fine-tuning what is already in the application is never billed.

 

The application stays yours

At handover you get the source code and instructions on how to run the application. Any other programmer can take it over anytime without starting from scratch. Nothing of yours is locked in with me.
