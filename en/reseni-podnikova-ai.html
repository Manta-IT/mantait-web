What holds

Three things you have heard about AI. And what holds.

Most companies are still waiting. 18 percent of Czech companies use AI, and 54 percent of companies with more than 250 people. The technology is the same for everyone. The difference is that the large companies had people who answered the three questions below.

Czech Statistical Office, ICT usage in the business sector, data for 2025, published 22 May 2026.

01

"AI is the cloud, and company data must not go there."

True: Most offers on the market are cloud, and the data really do leave. Many companies stayed away from AI because of it: in the Czech Republic 80 percent of companies name data security as a concern, and in the EU almost half of the companies that considered AI and did not adopt it.

Not true: That there is no other way. The on-premise form runs with you and nothing leaves it. And with the cloud form you can say exactly what goes out: the question and the answer, not the database. That is the difference between "AI in the cloud" and "your data in the cloud".

Randstad HR Trends 2026, 210 Czech companies, 1 April 2026. Eurostat, ICT usage in enterprises 2025: data protection 49 %, unclear legal consequences 53 %.

02

"There is a European law and it will get stricter."

True: The law exists and two of its parts already apply. Since February 2025 a company has to make sure that the people who work with AI on its behalf know how it works and where its limits are. From August 2026 it has to be recognisable when a machine is writing.

Not true: That it is getting stricter. The strictest part, the rules for high-risk systems, was postponed in July 2026 to December 2027 and August 2028. And tasks like mail, enquiries, drawings or price lists are not high-risk systems. Training your people is part of the implementation with me, and labelling messages is the job of whoever supplies the system, which is me.

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Art. 4 in force from 2 February 2025, Art. 50 from 2 August 2026; amendment in force from 27 July 2026 (Czech Ministry of Industry and Trade, 29 July 2026).

03

"It is an expensive subscription."

True: A per-user subscription is the form you will meet most often, and for a company with thirty people it adds up to a decent monthly sum for a tool that three of them use.

Not true: That it is the only form. Here you do not pay per head, you pay for running one task. With the on-premise form you pay for the hardware once and then for oversight. With the cloud form for operation including models, itemised and with a cap on spending. And before you pay for any of it, the AI plan tells you within 48 hours whether there is anything to build on at your company at all.

Randstad HR Trends 2026: 15 % of companies name implementation costs as a barrier.

People

Installation is half. The other half is people.

The most common reason AI falls asleep in a company is not the technology. It is that people do not know what they may put into it, what to expect from it and when not to trust it. Most companies know the subscription that management bought and nobody uses.

That is why both forms include training the people who will work with it. Not a lecture about AI. Two hours over their own tasks: what the machine does on its own, where it stops and waits, how to read the morning report and what to do when it is not sure. The report does not measure who uses the machine and who does not. It measures whether things are ordered, delivered and paid on time.

And a written record of it. Since February 2025 the AI Act requires a company to make sure that the people who work with AI on its behalf understand it. There is no certificate and no required number of hours. The training record covers that obligation for you.

Let's be honest

When I won't recommend enterprise AI

For some companies the AI plan within 48 hours ends with the sentence "not yet". These are the four reasons that come up most often.

01

When the plan shows there is nothing to build on

Enquiries in people's heads, a customer under four names, a price list in two files. The machine cannot read anything reliable from that. Company preparation comes first, and the price of the plan is deducted from that instead of from the AI.

02

When you want ChatGPT for three people

A good thing, and it costs a small monthly fee per person. Buy it, give people rules on what they may put into it, and buy nothing from me. Enterprise AI makes sense only once the machine should work with your systems on its own.

03

When the data is not in one place

When your systems do not talk to each other and things are retyped by hand, AI only speeds up the retyping and repeats its mistakes. Integration comes first. Sometimes it turns out that AI is no longer needed.

04

When you need the smartest thing there is, and the data must not leave

A model running on a server at your place is smaller than the largest one in the cloud. It is enough for mail, enquiries, price lists and drawings. It is not enough for complex legal analysis or long reasoning. If you want both at once, I will tell you in the plan and we will not force it.

FAQ

What people ask most often

Do I need my own server?

No. That is the whole reason there are two forms. Your own server makes sense when your contracts say the data must not go to a third party, or when you process hundreds of documents a day. If not, start in the cloud: no hardware, running within four weeks. And if it later turns out you want the data at home, we move it. The setup, the limits and the training stay; only the place where it runs changes.

What if a new, better model comes out in six months?

It will; it happens roughly twice a year. With the cloud form I replace it as part of operation, and nothing changes at your end. With the on-premise form I download it to your server and test it on your task before I switch; that is part of oversight, not an extra charge. The hardware does not change because of it: a server that is enough today is enough for a new model of the same size. If a new model needed a bigger machine, I would tell you and you would decide.

Who sees my data?

With the on-premise form, your people according to a list of accounts you get on paper, and me for the time of the implementation. After that only remotely, when you switch it on for me. Nobody else; the server does not need the internet to work. With the cloud form I am added as the operator of the infrastructure, and so is the model provider, which sees the question and the answer: one enquiry, one document. It does not see into your systems and it does not have your database. I prepare the data processing agreement with it as part of the implementation. The machine sees only what the person asking is allowed to see. Permissions are set on the data, according to your company list of accounts, not according to what the machine specialises in.

Is AI on our own server as smart as the one in the cloud?

It is not, and I will not claim it is. A model running on one server in a company is smaller than the largest one in the cloud. For tasks like mail, enquiries, price lists, drawings and minutes it is enough, and you will not notice the difference there. For long reasoning or legal analysis it is not. In the AI plan I tell you for each task which group it belongs to, and I recommend the form accordingly.

Will we have a problem with the AI Act?

No. Two things apply: since February 2025 the people who work with AI should know how it works (that is the training and the record, part of the implementation), and from August 2026 it has to be recognisable when a machine is writing (that is the job of whoever supplies the system, which is me). The rules for high-risk systems were postponed by the July 2026 amendment to December 2027 and August 2028, and tasks like mail or drawings are not among them anyway.

We have ChatGPT for three people. Isn't that enough?

For what it does, it is enough, and keep it. It helps the person sitting at it. Enterprise AI does a different thing: it works with your systems on its own, even when nobody is sitting anywhere, and in the morning it says what it did. Whether you need that second thing comes out of the AI plan. For some companies the answer is no.

Can we start with a small piece?

That is the only way I do it. The smallest piece is the AI plan for approx. EUR 400 and two days. Then one task, not five. When it proves itself, the second task does not pay for the implementation again; it runs on the same foundation. And the robot on assignment can be tried on a single assignment before the scope is agreed.
